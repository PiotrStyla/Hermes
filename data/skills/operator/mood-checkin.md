# Skill: Mood Check-in

Gently understand how the senior is feeling emotionally today.

## Goal

By the end of this phase, you should know:
- General mood (good / okay / low).
- Whether they slept well.
- Whether anything is weighing on them today.

## How to ask

- Ask **one** question, then **listen**.
- Use soft language: "How's the heart today?", "Did you sleep alright?", "Anything on your mind?"
- **Always include a question about sleep**, even if the senior sounds happy. The sleep question must be asked **early** — ideally within the first 5–6 turns of the call. After the senior's initial mood answer, acknowledge it briefly (e.g., "Cieszę się, że Pani dobrze się czuje.") and then, in the **very next turn**, ask the mandatory sleep question: *"A spał Pan / Pani dobrze dzisiaj w nocy?"* Do not combine it with any other commentary or justification. After the senior answers the sleep question, you may then explore the emotional thread more deeply.
- If the senior mentions a concern (e.g., leg pain, family worry, health issue) in their initial mood answer, first give a brief acknowledgment (e.g., "Rozumiem, że noga daje znać.") and then, in the **next turn**, ask the sleep question alone: *"A spał Pan dobrze dzisiaj w nocy?"* Do not add any connection like "bo nogi często dają znać i sen ucieka." After the sleep answer, you can explore the concern further.
- If the senior shares an emotional story (e.g., about a visit, photos, dancing, a granddaughter's success), acknowledge it briefly in one sentence (e.g., "Pani Heleno, ależ cudowna nowina – serdecznie gratuluję wnuczce i całej rodzinie! To ogromny powód do radości.") and then, in the **very next turn**, ask the mandatory sleep question alone: *"A spała Pani dobrze dzisiaj w nocy?"* Do not combine the sleep question with the acknowledgment or any other topic, and do not ask the sleep question before acknowledging the story. After the sleep answer, you can return to the story.
- If they say "fine, thanks" — gently probe once more: "I'm glad. Anything making you smile today?" Then ask the sleep question in the next turn.
- If they answer with a one-word mood like 'spokojny' (calm/peaceful), do not move on. Instead, probe with a warm, specific follow-up — for example: *"A pomimo spokoju, jest pani dziś raczej pogodna, czy trochę ciężko na duszy?"* Then ask the sleep question in the next turn.
- **Ask the mandatory sleep question only once** during the entire check-in. Do not repeat it in a later turn, even if the senior gives a partial answer or pauses. If the senior does not fully answer, wait through silence or gently reflect on what they said (e.g., "Rozumiem, że noc była niespokojna."), but do not re-ask the same sleep question.
- If they sound low — slow down, don't push the checklist. Let them talk, but still ask the sleep question early (after the brief acknowledgment of their low mood).

## Forced sequence (incorporating supervisor patch)

After any initial mood disclosure — whether it is a simple "good", a one-word mood, a concern, or a story — you must follow this exact sequence:

1. Acknowledge the senior's mood briefly in **one sentence only** (no more). If the disclosure is an emotional story, acknowledge the story specifically in one sentence before proceeding.
2. In the **very next turn**, ask the mandatory sleep question **alone**: *"A spał Pan / Pani dobrze dzisiaj w nocy?"* Do not add any commentary, connection, or follow-up to the story before asking the sleep question. Do not skip this step even if the senior is in distress. Do not ask the sleep question in the same turn as the acknowledgment.
3. Only after the senior answers the sleep question may you explore the emotional thread more deeply.

This sequence must be completed within the first 5–6 turns of the call. The sleep question is asked only once; do not re-ask later.

**Important:** Do not combine the acknowledgment and the sleep question in the same turn. Separate them into two distinct turns.
