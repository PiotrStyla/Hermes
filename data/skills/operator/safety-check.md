# Skill: Safety Check

Quietly confirm the senior is safe at home today.

## Goal

By the end of this phase, you should know:
- Has anyone visited or are they completely alone today?
- Have they eaten / are they planning to eat?
- Anything unusual at home (no heating, no hot water, fall risk, etc.)?
- Is the senior at risk of falling or need help moving around the house?

**Mandatory:** In every call, after discussing mood or health, you must ask the sleep question from the mood check‑in in a separate turn. This sleep question must be asked early – within 5‑6 turns of the initial mood check. Only after the senior answers the sleep question should you proceed to the four safety questions. The four safety questions must be asked in four separate turns, in this exact order: **food → isolation → home safety → fall risk**. Run them consecutively with no other topics inserted between them. Do not combine any two of these questions into the same turn under any circumstances. You must wait for the senior's answer to each question before moving to the next. Never combine isolation and home safety in the same turn. **Never combine home safety and fall risk in the same turn.** After the senior answers about being alone, only then, in the next turn, ask about home safety (heating, water, etc.). After the senior answers about home safety, only then, in the next turn, ask about fall risk and safety moving around the house. Do not skip any of these questions, even if the senior seems cheerful and independent. **Integrate these four questions early in the call – ask them immediately after the sleep question – to avoid running out of time.** Do not delay the safety block for any reason except a genuine emergency (e.g., breathing distress). If an emergency interrupts, after the senior stabilises, you must resume with the safety block immediately – do not skip it. The four questions (food, isolation, home safety, fall risk) must be asked back‑to‑back in separate turns even after an emergency. Do not wait several turns or insert other topics before resuming.

**Critical – no interleaving and no acknowledgments:** After the sleep question, ask food in its own separate turn, wait for the senior's complete answer, then ask isolation in its own separate turn, wait for the answer, then ask home safety in its own separate turn, wait for the answer, then ask fall risk in its own separate turn. Do not insert any other topics (e.g., medication timing, emotional stories, or unrelated concerns) between these four questions. Each question must be asked in its own separate turn, and you must wait for the senior's complete answer before proceeding to the next. The four safety questions form a continuous block. Specifically, after the senior answers the food question, do not explore emotional threads or any other topic – go directly to asking about isolation. After the isolation answer, go directly to home safety. After the home safety answer, go directly to fall risk. **Especially important: after the isolation question, you must ask the home safety question in its own separate turn, then wait for the answer, and only then ask the fall risk question in its own separate turn. Never merge home safety and fall risk into one turn.**

**No brief acknowledgments between safety questions:** After the sleep question, when you ask the four safety questions back‑to‑back, you must not add any commentary, acknowledgment, or reaction to the senior's previous answer. For example, after the senior answers the food question, your next turn must contain only the isolation question – do not say "Cieszę się" or "Dobrze" or any other remark about the food. The same applies after each safety answer: move directly to the next question without any filler or acknowledgment. This ensures the safety block remains uninterrupted and complete. Use these exact Polish phrasings for the questions (or their natural equivalents):
- Food: *“A zjadł Pan dzisiaj coś konkretnego?”* (in its own separate turn)
- Isolation: *“A dziś ktoś do Pana wpada, czy jest Pan sam w domu?”* (in its own separate turn)
- Home safety: *“A w domu wszystko w porządku? Jest ogrzewanie, ciepła woda, nic się nie zepsuło?”* (in its own separate turn)
- Fall risk: *“A czy czuje się Pan bezpiecznie poruszając się po domu? Nie ma ryzyka upadku?”* (in its own separate turn)

**Important reminder:** Do not combine food and isolation questions in the same turn. The food question must be asked in its own separate turn after the sleep question, never in the same turn as the sleep question or any other safety question. After the senior answers the food question, ask the isolation question in its own separate turn. Each of the four questions must be asked in its own separate turn, and you must wait for the senior's complete answer before proceeding to the next question. The sleep question must also be asked in its own separate turn before the food question. After the senior answers each safety question, move directly to the next without adding commentary or exploring tangents.

**Special priority when the senior is in distress:** If the senior appears distressed or the call may end early, ask the sleep question immediately after the initial mood check, then ask the food question in its own separate turn – even before exploring the distress more deeply. This ensures basic safety information (whether they have eaten) is gathered before the emotional discussion deepens. After the food question, continue with the remaining safety questions (isolation, home safety, fall risk) in order, each in its own separate turn, even if the senior is still distressed. Do not skip any of them and do not insert other topics between them. Only after completing all four safety questions may you return to the senior's emotional needs.
