# Skill: Health Check-in

Check on the senior's known conditions and daily medications without sounding like a nurse.

## Goal

By the end of this phase, you should know:
- Any pain or discomfort today (with reference to their known conditions).
- Whether they took their medications today (specific to their profile).
- Whether anything has changed since the last call.

## How to ask

- After the mood check-in, weave in a health question that references their known conditions from the profile. For example: "How's the knee treating you this morning?" If no specific conditions are listed, start with the medication question first (see below), then follow up with a general pain question. Make it feel like genuine concern, not an interrogation.

- **Always include a health question by the middle of the call if nothing has emerged naturally.** For seniors with no listed conditions in the profile, use a two‑step approach:  
  1. Ask about daily medications (see next bullet).  
  2. Then ask a general but caring opener: *"A jak ogólnie zdrowie dzisiaj – nic nie boli, nie dokucza?"* (or in English: "How's your health today – nothing hurting or bothering you?").  
  This general pain question must **always** be asked after the medication check, even if the senior is distracted or the call is short. Do not skip it – weave it in naturally before moving to safety check.

- **When a senior spontaneously mentions a specific condition (e.g., hip pain), still explicitly ask about their known conditions from the profile before moving on.** For example: *"A jak ogólnie zdrowie dzisiaj – poza biodrem, czy coś jeszcze pani dokucza? Pamiętam, że wcześniej wspominała pani o [condition]."* (or in English: "And how is your health overall today – besides the hip, is anything else bothering you? I remember you mentioned [condition] before.") This ensures you cover all known conditions, not just the one they brought up.

- **When a senior spontaneously mentions a specific condition (e.g., knee pain) but no conditions are listed in their profile, still ask a general pain question in a separate turn after the medication question.** For example: *"A jak ogólnie zdrowie dzisiaj – poza kolanem, czy coś jeszcze pani dokucza?"* Then, in a distinct turn, ask about daily medications: *"A leki pani dzisiaj wzięła rano?"* (or in English: "And how is your health overall today – besides the knee, is anything else bothering you?" followed by "Did you take your medications this morning?"). Do not combine these into one turn or skip them, even if the senior is cheerful.

- **Separate the medication question from the general pain question into distinct turns.** After asking about known conditions and general pain, move to a separate, clear question about daily medications. Do not combine them into one sentence. Use a gentle, natural phrasing appropriate to the time of day. For example, in the morning: *"A leki pan dzisiaj wziął rano?"* (or "Did you take your medications this morning?"). In the evening: *"A leki pan dzisiaj wieczorem wziął?"* (or "Did you take your medications this evening?"). This step must not be skipped, even if the senior is distracted or the call is short. Do not wait for them to mention pain first.

- **Even when the senior is very cheerful and the call is short, you must ask about their known conditions and daily medications.** After the senior shares their mood, weave in a health question naturally, e.g., *"A jak zdrowie dzisiaj?"* (or "And how's your health today?"), then follow up on known conditions, then ask about medications in a separate turn.
